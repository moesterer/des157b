Capstone Project Proposal

My topic is going to surround the reverse aging research and breakthroughs that have been happening in the past decade. Although right now the research agrees that while reverse aging is a buzzword and the actual science is more focused on targeting diseases that appear later in life, it is still an interesting concept to tackle especially if the promise of extending a human life by decades comes to fruition and how that would impact our society. Anything new, rare, and highly valued, like this reverse aging technology, no matter how well intentioned, will likely be hard to obtain by individuals unless those individuals are very rich or have connections to people who are very rich. The technology would be utilized fully and exploited by those who can afford it, CEOs, billionaires, celebrities, and politicians. This sort of technology might not ever trickle down to the middle class, much less those stricken with poverty. I plan to examine this ‘what if’ worst case scenario of the repercussions of this longevity technology in the hands of the privileged few. Particularly the impact on politics so this project would fall into a combination of the Health and Politics categories. The two lenses I plan to utilize are primarily Speculative Design, what would our societies look like 100 years in the future if this technology became available in the next 5 years, with a clear Dystopian Narrative as I will be examining with a bad case imagined track. If politicians and the rich have a monopoly on human lifespan that would mean they are around longer to make decisions, they can stay longer in their seats of power, there would be less change because there is less turnover of positions filled and decision makers. This might even lead to political agendas that continue this consolidation and push those without access even further down and make it increasingly harder to grasp. They could even manipulate public sentiment to an extreme by changing laws so that the presidential terms are longer because the presidents live longer. Eventually it could lead to a society where those in power seem to live forever and are considered all powerful (a sentiment purposefully perpetuated in schools) because of this while those who remember that this is not true and the origins of their lifespans die quicker and history is thus re-written by those still around to tell it. Essentially, in this dystopian world, the country would live ruled over by an immortal overlord while the majority of the population maintains natural human lifespans, dying out too quickly to make a difference and push for change in comparison.

One of the features I imagine would be a series of newspapers, with indicative headlines, shown chronologically introducing the future society. It would include headlines with the main point, but have actual article (self-written) text essentially speaking about breakthroughs, public outcry/reactions, marking the progression of society, laws, and common sense until the user reaches this ‘current’ modern dystopia. The main part of the project is something I’m not set on yet. Would it be an overview of how the society works, or would it be focused on a single normal citizen’s life that from our perspective has little changed as a result of the past that seems strange and off-putting to the audience? Painting a day in the life type of citation with scenes, things to inspect, dialogue, with an ominous undertone which will provoke thought about an extreme direction our society could go is the goal. I liked the strong feeling of shock and uncomfortableness that was evoked in the Judicial AI trainer simulation shown in class was a huge inspiration. That emotional and shocking factor to make it personal is an element I want to emulate although I’m not sure of the specifics of the storyline as of yet. Making sure that all the outcomes follow historical research on this monopoly of technology and tyrannical world leaders consolidating power so that the thought experiment is still rooted in reality will be key to this. I’m not sure if I will have the user make decisions that could change trajectories or outcomes of the narrative, or if just timing the narrative while giving the user things to examine or read closer in the scenes to inform themselves about this future world is the direction I will be going in (like ‘picking up’ an object to see it in more detail). If I were to implement this picking up detail using the library Atropos I used in a previous project to make items more 3D, that would be interesting and heighten the interactivity of the project.

My target audience would most likely be the younger generations who will inherit the future shaped by today’s political systems. Specifically college students and young adults who are interested in technology, ethics, healthcare, politics, and inequality. Politically aware 18-25 year old students who are anxious about wealth inequality, aging political leadership, billionaire power, and unequal access to healthcare innovation. I’m asking the question of what happens if people already in power are no longer limited by age, illness, or natural generational turnover and so the audience is young enough to imagine themselves living in and through the future I am designing. Which makes the project more personal than abstract. They would likely understand both sides of the issue as medical breakthroughs are a source of hope but there is always the danger of unequal access and exploitative applications. They might not be experts in longevity science but will recognize the pattern of new technologies often reaching the wealthy first while everyone else waits, pays more, and is still typically excluded. An interactive warning will be more effective than a science lecture type of feel. At this age the audience is politically aware but often feels powerless in the face of the especially powerful, whether than be wealthy or politically involved individuals or unfair systems as they are not yet part of the workforce or in positions where they have any direct say. The fact that in this dystopian future ordinary people remain disposable seems not that far of a stretch as it is something our generation has become increasingly aware of.
